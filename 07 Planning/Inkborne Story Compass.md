# The Inkborne Trilogy — Story Compass

> **Working decision document.** Only what the author has *confirmed* lives here. Anything not in this file is NOT decided — and that's fine. No pressure to decide it today.
>
> **How to use this:** every future decision (twist, scene, villain, magic rule) must serve this compass. If it doesn't, it goes in the parking lot — not the story. This filter is what ends the "thousand directions" feeling.

---

## 1. The feeling this trilogy exists to create

*Author's taste rules, in her own words — these are the selection filters for every choice from here on:*

- A love story like Xaden/Violet, Zevander/Maevyth — where readers genuinely **lose hope that a happily ever after could even exist**.
- Odds like ACOTAR's final battle — going in believing it may be their last day… and still getting the happy ending.
- A heroine who is **not a wallflower**, and visibly grows in skill *and* character as the story progresses.
- Side characters everyone falls in love with — not forgettable.
- A **bittersweet ending — but not that bitter.**
- An MMC who is a **real man**, not just the stock possessive fantasy archetype.
- Villains who are **not cartoonish** — they can show how truly evil they are.
- **No misunderstanding trope.** Ever.
- He falls first; she falls harder.
- No insta-lust.

**Working one-line compass:** Two people the world spent their whole lives trying to break become each other's proof that they can't be.

## 2. The MMC — Kyrell Mavaros (LOCKED CORE)

- He can be possessive and jealous — that is important in a romantasy. But never to the point where "you are mine" is all he is.
- **Model: Xaden / Rhysand — NOT Tamlin.**
  - Tamlin protects her *from* her life. Rhys and Xaden protect her *right to live it*.
  - His "you are mine" is an **oath** ("no one will ever hurt you"), never a **verdict** ("you belong to me").
- He treats her like a queen. He would **fight to keep them together**, **burn the world if it means she lives**, and be **ready to burn himself if there is no way out**.
- **His arc:** a self-made slum orphan from the Sinks — control is his survival scar, not a hobby. His instinct will be to cage, hide, guard her. The story is him learning to **fight beside her, not for her** — to trust her to stand in her own fire.
- **Armor comes off:** the gloves, the masks, the Sovereign. Being truly seen is the only thing that has ever made him safe. Adira is the only person alive who can read him — his ink micro-tells, his junction fatigue. His love story is being *known*.

## 3. Taste guardrails — the author's compass (from her inspos & anti-inspos)

> Every future decision must pass these. If a twist, death, or plot beat violates a guardrail, it goes in the parking lot.

### ❌ Anti-models (never repeat these failures)
- **No sequel collapse.** *(Crimson Moth B2, Powerless B2+)* Books 2 & 3 must be as strong as Book 1 → structure the whole series before drafting.
- **No MMC who stops fighting for them.** *(Kai)* When they're separated or it gets hard, Kyrell fights his way back to her. Always.
- **No cheap, illogical deaths.** *(Powerless — the brother)* A death must be earned, logical, necessary, and set up. Never just to make the reader sad. This **overrides** any earlier "Book 1 must hurt" framing: real fear comes from capable villains and real costs — not gratuitous killing.
- **No twists you can see from miles away.** *(Mages and Mischief)* Twists must be **surprising yet inevitable** — never random (illogical), never announced (predictable).
- **No abrupt romantic pivot.** *(ACOTAR Tamlin→Rhys)* No sudden switch of love interest. One MMC, a slow burn earned over the whole series.

### ✅ Models (what the series should taste like)
- **Fourth Wing** — plot and romance both excellent, in balance; high stakes; competent heroine.
- **ACOTAR (overall)** — the scale of threat; a love that feels impossible.
- **Daughter of No Worlds** — an ending that makes you *cry*; a tragedy in a secondary thread executed so nicely it enriches the whole; a "real man" MMC (Max) who's an equal partner, never a cage.
- **One Dark Window** — gothic atmosphere; a unique, eerie magic system — *and the author wished it had MORE romance* → romance is central, never secondary.
- **Broken Souls** — a duology whose arc and ending land completely.

## 4. The FMC — Adira Solari (DRAFT — awaiting author's reaction)

> Built from canon + the author's model (Violet). NOT locked — react, change, reject.

- **The flavor:** Violet — semi-free, semi-duty-bound; forced onto a path she didn't choose, yet fully accepts it and becomes the most legendary at it. Plus a fierce will (Tisaanah) and the gothic girl with something secret inside.
- **Semi-free, semi-duty-bound (CANON):** free in her days and pursuits — except the binding duty of her station. She did **not** flee her betrothal or her clan. The path was chosen for her, and she fully accepted it, shouldered it, and became **Master Wayfinder of Solara at 23.** The shipwreck was **not an escape** — she was sailing to her betrothed's clan per tradition when the storm struck.
- **The islands' truth (CORRECTED):** islanders are **all unmarked** and know **nothing** of ink or tattoo-magic — only the High Elders even know the mainland exists, and they have never seen ink with their own eyes. So on the isles, Adira's bare skin is **completely normal**. "Unmarked / blank / less" is **not** a concept at home. She was never lesser — she was simply the Wayfinder, the daughter, the bride-to-be.
- **What the mainland sees (and she collides with):** on the mainland, ink = power, rank, worth. A bare-skinned adult is alien — read as a blank, a nobody, a canvas to be written on. *(Working read: if "The Unmarked Bride" alias is ever used, it is a **mainland** label — islanders would never think it.)*
- **Her collision, not her backstory:** she does not arrive carrying shame about her skin. She arrives whole, and the mainland's misreading of her is the *new* injury she must refuse.
- **The lie she must refuse (only bites on the mainland):** worth = how perfectly she fills the roles assigned to her. On the islands she mastered every role and was fulfilled. On the mainland she is handed a degrading role — *blank, lesser, object* — and the arc is her refusing to accept it.
- **The truth the series proves:** she was never less; she simply belongs to no ink hierarchy. The one person in the story who was never written on is the one person who can never be owned by the mainland's system. Her freedom isn't granted — she was always outside their claim.
- **The symmetry with Kyrell:** he is the most *controlled* man alive — 8 glyphs, masks, gloves, unreadable. She reads *people*, not ink — which is exactly why she can read his micro-tells and junction fatigue. He hides behind the very system that marked him; she is the one person that system never touched.
- **Why he falls first:** she's the first person who saw *him*, not the Shadow Sovereign.
- **Why she falls harder:** once she finally trusts the one man who never tried to own her, she loves with her whole self.

## 5. Book 1 — Opening premise (PARTIALLY LOCKED)

> The author's story-in-her-head. CONFIRMED items are locked; OPEN items still under discussion.

### ✅ CONFIRMED (locked)
- Arranged betrothal: Adira ↔ Theron of Clan Korvos. Per tradition she sails to his clan escorted by **5 guards (incl. Ronan)** + a **handmaiden**.
- **Catastrophe = STORM** (not an attack). No enemy knows they're there — no conspiracy in Chapter 1. Everyone dies except **Ronan, the handmaiden, and Adira.**
- They **wake on a deserted shore** — no one there. Their first, logical assumption: they drifted onto one of the **Five Isles**.
- **Discovery is gradual and earned.** They do NOT stumble onto anything conveniently. They move logically: look for dry clothes → food → scout the area → find shelter (and even shelter may not come on the first night).
- Only as they progress inland do they see people who **look different** and bear **tattoos** — and slowly realize this is **not one of the isles** but an entirely foreign land. (Islanders know nothing of ink; seeing tattooed strangers is alien → confusing → frightening as they learn what the marks can do.)
- The run-in with Kyrell's operation comes **only after real days** of surviving, hiding, and observing — timeline open, nothing rushed.
- **The two companions are full people with their own reactions**, not props: Ronan (sarcastic morale anchor, warrior, fiercely protective) and the handmaiden — **Sera** (working name in the drafts; locked character: the competent, steady organizer-healer who carries Adira's old life. Full identity & backstory still open).

### 🔓 OPEN (working notes)
- Mechanics of the theft / capture / why Kyrell spares them → later. Principles: the theft must **cost him something real**; sparing her must be **earned** (usefulness, curiosity — never insta-attraction); Adira must **not reveal** her wayfinding or her value too early.

## 6. The theft & the pursuit (PARTIALLY LOCKED)

### ✅ LOCKED (author-confirmed)
- **The thieves get away clean.** They are NOT caught mid-theft — they escape and vanish first.
- **But Adira's Mother's Locket is dropped at the scene** (cord snaps in the scuffle). **Kyrell finds it and keeps it — privately.** Only he + the reader know. Adira discovers it's missing later — grief nearly as sharp as the shipwreck (her mother's, the only thing of home, the thing the legend says guides her home). She does NOT know he has it.
- **Kyrell doesn't know what the locket is** — to him it reads as an exotic sealed keepsake (volcanic obsidian, mother-of-pearl). But he traffics in secrets; he can tell it's *more* than it looks. He keeps it out of **curiosity** — the seed of "bring her alive" before any attraction exists. Only Adira knows what's inside.
- **Kyrell is already investigating Thalor's armada** — he's smart; his men are already pulling that thread. So the theft must NOT be how he learns about the armada (that would make him look stupid). It costs him something **else**.
- **Kyrell is NOT on scene for the theft** — he stays **mysterious / off-stage**. The pursuit is run by his empire (his men, his network — dockmasters, paid eyes). The first time Adira sees him is when she is brought before him.
- **Pacing cap:** the escape + pursuit = **~2–3 chapters max**. By roughly **chapter 5 (~15% mark)**, Adira stands before Kyrell. Book 1 has much more to cover.
- **"The sea is hers, but not the land" — LOCKED theme of the act.** She is absolute on water, helpless on land; he is the reverse. The whole first act keeps them each in the *other's* territory — which is why they need each other before they like each other.

### ✅ LOCKED — the theft's cost & the Thalor guardrail (author-confirmed)
- **What makes it "kill-worthy":** **escalating humiliation + one of Kyrell's men injured.**
  - The thieves slip his net **not once but twice** — by the third report, the Shadow Sovereign has been made a fool on his own coast by three nobodies. In his world, embarrassment is blood.
  - **One of his men is injured** in the escape (not killed). Underworld code: those who bleed under his command are his to answer for. The injury keeps it personal without planting an unearned corpse the romance must later reckon with.
- **SPARING LOGIC (what makes him not kill her):** pride says kill, but his reports say "bare skin, no ink, no coin, an islander girl who moves like water and outran my best men" — plus the sealed obsidian locket in his own hand. He needs to **understand** her before he decides. Curiosity + competence wins over wounded pride. (No insta-lust — this is all appetite of the mind.)
- **THALOR GUARDRAIL (locked):** Thalor must remain a **smart, promising antagonist** — the *reason* for his armada must NOT be revealed early, and Kyrell must NOT crack it early. Kyrell's men pull the thread and meet **dead ends** — timber that's accounted for, shipwrights who know nothing, false leads. Kyrell *senses* the wrongness but cannot prove it. This makes Thalor worthy of Book 1 and protects the reveal for when it hurts most.

### ✅ LOCKED — his real motive: the third source of Prime Ink (author-confirmed)
- **The assay & the finding:** Kyrell keeps the locket. He opens it (the mother's warning — *never open it, never spill it* — violated without Adira's knowledge), takes a minuscule sample, and assays it. His organization is the only one on the continent that can fingerprint raw Prime Ink by **material source-signature** — because they alone have found and tapped **2 sources** (canon source #1 = the Weeping Deep under Veridia; source #2 = *placeholder — unassigned, likely the north*). Raw ink is inert unbound, so this is material analysis, not magic — consistent with the magic bible. It fits Kyrell's public role (he runs the **Assay** & Bullion House).
- **The result takes days:** the sample matches **NEITHER** known source → it comes from a **THIRD, unknown source of Prime Ink** → and the locket's bearer is the only living lead to it. That is his true motive for keeping her — and it is **private and invisible** to Adira.
- **TWO-TRACK STRUCTURE (locked):**
  - **Track A — public (what Adira experiences):** the spectacle, the claimed debt, being put to work. His *cover*, and genuinely how he handles them — keep her alive and pliable while the real work runs.
  - **Track B — private (what she can't see):** the assay runs in the background for a few days. When the no-match result lands, his interest sharpens from "debtors" to "the only lead to a third source." In single POV, Adira/reader only feel him grow more attentive without knowing why.
- **The Thalor guardrail holds:** a third source *exists* ≠ knowing *where*. The assay reveals nothing about an island chain, a gyre, or unmarked people. Thalor (holding Koa's wind-bearings) knows *where* but Kyrell doesn't → two men circle the same secret from opposite sides, neither knowing the other has it. Thalor stays a worthy antagonist; nothing reveals early.
- **Future payoff (parked, not planted):** the third source = Adira's home (Mount Kora). When Kyrell learns the thing he's been hunting his whole career is her sacred mountain, it becomes a moral hinge: his empire's dream vs. her people. Ties to his locked arc (would he burn the world for her — or refuse to burn *hers*?). For Books 2–3.
- **The locket is now a clock:** Adira knows what's inside (only she + her mother did). When she realizes it's gone, she's grieving *and* terrified the secret is loose. He has already opened it. The reveal will hit like a wave.

### 🔓 OPEN
- **THE 2 KNOWN SOURCES (LOCKED — found in canon):** ① **The Weeping Deep** (the Sacred Wellhead under Veridia's High Citadel — official Crown source). ② **The Private Northern Seep** — a secret source operating deep in the mountains of the **Ironcrag Duchy** (northern mining march), worked by secret mountain highwaymen. *(Note: the seep is canonized in `03 Worldbuilding/The Valerian Empire/The Four Duchies.md`, not in Kyrell's character file.)* The locket's Mountain Blood matches NEITHER → the third, unknown source.
- **LOCKED — when Adira notices the locket is gone:** within one day (post-theft) the loss hits her — but the chaos of the capture and debt buries it. She cannot act on it. It resurfaces as a thread when things settle.
- Timeline of Track B landing (capture ≈ ch5 → assay result ≈ a few days later → his first real "assignment" to her after that).

## 7. POV STRUCTURE (LOCKED — changes how we plot everything)

- **Single POV: Adira only.** Fourth Wing style. The reader experiences the entire world through her eyes and never leaves her head.
- **Consequences (all locked):**
  - Kyrell's inner world is **never directly shown** — we only see *evidence* of his feelings through his actions, choices, and restraint. The reader reads him the way Adira does: wondering. (His private "he falls first" arc is conveyed through what he *does*, not what he thinks.)
  - His off-screen movements and knowledge are **placeholder** — resolved later, never contradicting what Adira sees. Rule: *he can never do anything on-page that Adira would have witnessed.*
  - **Thalor's armada secret stays fully intact** — the reader is never shown Kyrell confirming it, only his unreadable competence. Neither Adira nor the reader ever gets certainty → Thalor stays a worthy antagonist.
  - The **locket reveal stays dormant**: Adira doesn't know Kyrell has it → it surfaces only on the day she (and the reader) discover it together.
