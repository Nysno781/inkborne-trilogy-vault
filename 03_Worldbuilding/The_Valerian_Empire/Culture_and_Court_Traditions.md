# Mainland Culture, Etiquette & Court Traditions

---

## 1. High-Society Etiquette & Hospitality

* **The "Salt & Steel Hearth Pour" (Iron Hospitality):** A host lays a clean steel dagger flat across the table and pours two cups of tea/spirits from a single kettle, drinking first to prove no poison or ambush under his roof.
* **The "Five-Pace Hearth Boundary":** Guests must halt precisely five paces from the hearth chair until verbally invited (*"Take the hearth"*). Stepping closer uninvited justifies drawing steel.
* **The "Unclasped Cloak at the Threshold":** Surrendering one's wet wool travel cloak at the door signals peaceful entry; keeping it buttoned announces expected bloodshed.
* **The Four-Second Silence:** In banking and diplomacy, rushing to reply is viewed as vulgar desperation.

---

## 2. Mainland Romance Rites & High-Tension Customs

* **"The Mantle Drape" (Territorial Protection):** Draping a heavy, tailored wool longcoat or fur mantle over a woman's shoulders is an ancient feudal declaration: *"She is under my mantle; her safety is answered by my blood."*
* **"The Three-Dance Rule" (Ballroom Jealousy):** A lady may dance with an acquaintance *once* out of courtesy, *twice* if courted, but a *third dance* is an open declaration of betrothal or exclusive possession.
* **"The Favor of Steel":** Wearing a gentleman's signet, dagger, or hairpin on one's sash requires all other suitors to seek permission from the blade's owner before approaching.
* **"The Glove Strip" (Threshold of Bare-Skin Intimacy):** Because aristocrats wear leather gloves at all times as armor, touching someone's bare face, neck, or wrist with an ungloved hand in private is the ultimate threshold of surrender.

---

## 3. Life-Cycle & Funerary Rites

* **The Salt-Tongue Rite:** Moments after birth, coarse rock salt is placed on an infant's tongue to ward off river damp.
* **The Iron Vow Wedding:** At dusk, a couple's right wrists are bound by a solid cold-iron or silver chain sealed with molten red wax, remaining chained together until dawn.
* **The Ash-Wiping Rite (Death):** Before embalming, a priest rubs grey wood-ash across every tattoo on a corpse to blot out the magical circuit so the soul is not trapped between worlds.
